Planning the first classroom trial of MitoMapa

Classroom trial protocol · web 4.9.0 · Editable resource 4.8.7 · 27 October 2026.

Status: Classroom trial with learners pending. Use this sheet to check whether learners understand and can use the standalone website with the planned support. Trial results and evidence of educational impact are still pending. This guide describes features of the website. The editable resource remains at version 4.8.7.

Before the session

  1. Agree on a short route and its task. One 45-minute session on one island, or a route through two islands with a creative conclusion, may be enough. Choose the aim, the task and how to share it. You can start with any of the eight islands. Teachers choose the curriculum links and support for the group at the appropriate stage.
  2. Check island selection and navigation. Check that Map and List offer all eight islands, that the list appears first on mobile and that every story opens. Check the ‘Our work today’ instructions, the way into the Workshop and ‘Create and share’. Agree on the evidence to review. The collection of pieces and the reviewed route have separate progress records.
  3. Prepare for reading and listening. Open the story and try the narration controls for pausing, playback speed and ‘Gentle background sound’. Check headphones, connectivity and listening without background sound. In ‘Reading settings’, review text size, line spacing and the text width. The browser remembers these choices where local storage is available, independently of the learning stage. Clues link to passages of text. The recording is played separately in this version.
  4. Check the support and alternatives. Try supported reading, pauses and ways to respond by speaking, writing, drawing or performing. If you use the island names or piece games, test alternatives to dragging with touch, a click, the keyboard or a drop-down list. For geographical orientation, use ‘Locate the islands in words’. Discuss the cautions about historical names and images. The pottery pieces are recreations and the shell represents La Graciosa as a modern symbol. Learners can continue at any point in the games.
  5. Prepare to discuss and create. Try ‘Clue 1’, ‘Clue 2’, ‘See explanation’ and the link back to the passage in each challenge. Choose a short source and a question that helps learners compare it with the story. In ‘Create and share’, choose one or two islands, review the example and agree on an audience, format and improvement that learners can explain after reviewing their draft.

During the session

Record approximate timings to help adapt the session and note any instructions you had to explain. Use the timings to plan the activity. Allow time for pauses, another attempt or using support. Record what helps learners take part and any barrier that remains.

  • Finding an island observe whether learners know how to choose an island in Map or List and how to follow the activity instructions. If they need help, ask ‘What could you do now?’ and offer support promptly. Note which label or action needed explaining.
  • Using support and explaining choices note the clue, reading setting or alternative that helped learners continue and whether it was easy to return to the passage. Ask learners to explain a decision using words from the story, aloud or in the workbook. Use the questions to start a conversation. The questions record neither a self-assessment nor an automatic score for explanations.
  • Checking sources ask learners to identify the source and complete ‘The story says… The source says… We are still unsure about…’. Give help if needed and make the written note optional. Distinguish the supporting questions pupils have checked from the comparison they have reviewed with their teacher.
  • Reviewing creative work check whether learners can start with one or two islands, identify the intended audience and explain what came from the story, what they created and what they changed after the review. They can do the task without a complete collection of pieces. Creative work stays private.

If learners get stuck, offer an alternative and continue. Ask a brief question to see whether learners distinguish fictional story or illustration details from information supported by a source and recognise an uncertainty. The illustrations are modern recreations.

After the session

  1. Discuss what was clear, what made it hard to continue and what could change. You can download voluntary feedback comments without completing a rating scale.
  2. Review the agreed evidence and record completion of the route when appropriate. Review the work with learners. In the interactive Workbook, a saved draft, checked answers and a response marked as ready all have their own status, separate from the review with a teacher. Save a progress file if you will continue on another device. Clear progress before another learner or group uses the same browser.
  3. Choose one improvement to prioritise and decide how to check it in the next session. A positive comment or a correct answer alone cannot establish learning or educational impact.

Observation notes without identifiers or telemetry use this sheet to record your own observations. Leave out names, initials, email addresses, images, voices, device identifiers and extracts of work that could identify learners. Summarise the difficulties and support without grading or ranking people. Keep this sheet and individual progress records local. The protocol collects no data automatically.

Limits of this check a classroom trial with learners and evaluation with assistive technology users are still pending. Record the device, browser and support actually used and any checks you could not carry out. Checking WCAG conformance and educational impact requires separate evidence. Paragraph-by-paragraph playback of the recordings would require alignment and human review. The recording plays independently in this edition.

Use the browser’s Print option to print the observation sheet on its own. Fill it in on paper.
