MitoMapa Canario: eight islands, eight pieces

Example for the third cycle of Spanish primary education · four sessions · October 2026.

Authors: Jesica Fortes Regalado, Gloria Elsa González Martín and Cristian García Santana

About this guide

MitoMapa offers activities for several learning stages. Teachers choose competencies, assessment criteria, tasks and support for their group. This guide gives an example for the sixth year of Spanish primary education, in the third cycle. It can be adapted for the fifth year. Use the criteria and activities that fit your own group. The practical guide describes three options. Choose one island for about 45 minutes, this short route through two islands in four sessions of about 50 minutes, or the optional collection in eight to ten sessions of 35–50 minutes.

The editable project and the website

Editable learning object 4.8.9. The ELPX for eXeLearning contains 34 pages, 118 iDevices, 42 native games and 16 audio tracks. It includes stories, illustrations, activities and recordings for local use. It runs within eXeLearning. The teaching guidance is available in expandable sections.

Standalone website. The MitoMapa Canario website provides an interactive map and tools for reading, games and progress tracking. The last page of the ELPX links to the website as an optional extra. Opening it requires internet access. The Home, Teacher and Settings and progress controls mentioned in this guide are found on the website.

Run the four-session example

Plan four sessions of about 50 minutes each. Learners choose two of the eight islands. Combine listening, reading, games, discussion, comparing sources and creating a response. A classroom trial is still pending. Adapt timings to suit the group.

Plan for four sessions
SessionTeacher’s roleLearners’ role
1 · Choose and discoverFind out what learners know about the map. Introduce the museum piece, then agree on two islands and the support needed. Listen together and pause for discussion.Choose an island and find it on the map. Read or listen to the first story, answer the comprehension questions and explain one character’s decision.
2 · Read and compareSupport reading of the second story and show how to use passages to back up a comparison.Put events in order. Use the games and discussion to compare characters, conflicts or landscapes in the two stories.
3 · Check sources and createGive learners a short, accessible source on screen or in print. Help them distinguish documented information from invented details.Check a detail about heritage against a source. Draft a museum piece with a title, island, source and credits.
4 · Review and shareInvite questions and suggestions and help learners complete the agreed route.Review and share the museum piece. Explain what you have learned and what you would improve.

Suggested structure for each session. Allow 5 minutes to introduce the activity, 30 to explore or create, 10 to share and 5 to reflect. Adjust the timings or continue in another session if needed. Use the optional extensions separately from the short route.

Optional extension on the website Island names lets learners match seven historical names and the ‘No documented name’ card to an antique-style map. Drag the cards or select and place them using touch, a click or the keyboard. The map is a modern recreation and a starting point for checking sources. The game gives no pieces or grades. Use the sources to discuss what can be established about the names. Check the sources and uncertainties before using the game. Learners can complete the agreed route without playing it.

Support and participation

  • Keep the written story alongside Zeus Plasencia Carballo’s recording. Let pupils pause, reread, enlarge the text and listen with background sounds turned off.
  • Learners can answer aloud, dictate, write, explain a drawing or perform. They can work on their own, in pairs or in small groups with rotating roles.
  • Break instructions into steps. Model a response, introduce key vocabulary and read short sources with the group.
  • Learners can choose the island and form of their piece and use feedback from the games and group discussion.

Options for other groups

In early years and the first primary cycle, prioritise supported listening, discussion and explaining a drawing, using short texts. In the second cycle, compare fewer sources. In secondary education, devote more time to evaluating sources and writing in the available genres. The curriculum table below is for the third cycle of Spanish primary education. Adapt your curriculum choices to other groups.

In the ELPX, open the teaching guidance sections you need. The chapters group related content and allow you to start with any island.

If you use the website, select ‘Third primary cycle’ under the learning stage and reading settings on Home and agree on two islands. If you use the ELPX, use the support built into its chapters. In either format, the final piece includes a short creative retelling, a detail supported by a source and a proposal to care for heritage.

Use a compatible browser and, for listening, headphones or speakers. No account is required. The ELPX includes stories, activities and audio for local use. Research links and the standalone website require internet access. For offline work, provide a short printed or previously downloaded source that you have permission to use. The templates are optional.

Focus on understanding and improving the work. Listen to learners’ explanations, check how they distinguish sources from fictional details and discuss changes to their pieces. Offer questions and suggestions.

Learners use immediate feedback from the games, share a suggestion with someone else and reflect at the end. The passport can help them recall the work they agreed on. In Teacher → Settings and progress on the website, choose the islands and record a review of each island’s tasks. A route can show two agreed islands reviewed out of two. The collection of eight pieces remains separate. Record and review the eXeLearning passport by hand, separately from progress on the website.

Use the optional checklists and qualitative review sheet to collect evidence, record progress and agree a next step. Review the aspects worked on and accept spoken, written, visual or performed responses with the support learners need. The sheets calculate no scores, averages or grades. The percentage in the 42-game report is separate from assessment of the two-island route.

Where you assess curriculum criteria, use the evidence and official rubrics in line with your teaching plan, the Assessment Order of 31 May 2023 and the assessment and grading instructions for 2026–2027.

Reviewing creative work Use the criteria for the agreed stage and format. Primary tasks use the criteria for short creative work. Secondary genre criteria apply only to Secondary tasks. Mark an aspect ‘Not applicable’ if learners have not worked on it or it does not apply to an oral, visual or performed response. The checklists record observations without awarding points.

Optional website feedback

In ‘How was your expedition?’ on the standalone website, learners can select ratings, write comments or do both. Use ‘Download my feedback’ to save their responses. Comments can be downloaded on their own. If you choose to send feedback, attach the downloaded file to a message to sugerencias.mitomapacanario@gmail.com. Learners should ask their teacher or family for support. Leave out names and personal data. Sending feedback is manual and voluntary.
