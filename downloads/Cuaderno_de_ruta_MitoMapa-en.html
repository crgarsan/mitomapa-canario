My route workbook

Choose one or more islands and start wherever you like. Agree with your teacher what you will do, what support you need and how you will share your work.

You can respond by speaking, drawing, writing or performing. Use the sections that are part of your task and return to them whenever you need to.

Use this sheet alongside the website or the ELPX. Print it or adapt a copy. To print, press Ctrl+P (⌘+P on a Mac) or select Print in your browser. Fill in the sheet on paper, or adapt a copy for digital use.

Teachers adapt the tasks and support and link them to the curriculum for their group. This workbook can be used with any year group and any number of islands.

Read or listen and understand

Read or listen to the story. Pause, reread, listen again or ask for help whenever you need to.

What happens and what decision does a character make? Which detail from the story helps explain it?

A word, question or idea I want to discuss.

Consult a source if we agreed to

My question about the story, the island or its heritage.

Note the source title, author or institution, where you found it and the date you consulted it.

What information can I find? Note the detail and the page, section or passage where it appears.

Does the source record a legend, provide documented information or present a recreation? It may do more than one of these. What does the source support and what remains uncertain?

If you cannot find or confirm an answer, record that. If you agreed to use another source and it would help, compare what the sources tell you.

Prepare something to share

Choose what to make with your teacher. You could tell a story aloud, write a refrain or short story, explain a drawing, perform a scene or create a museum piece with an explanation.

Who is my work for? What do I want to communicate and in what format?

My idea, outline, sketch or plan. I can add a sheet or explain it aloud.

What have I taken from the story? What is backed up by sources? What have I added myself? For any materials I reuse, I record the author, source, terms of use and changes I made.

Review and share

Share a draft, drawing, explanation or rehearsal. Use the review sheet and choose the sections that are relevant to the agreed task.

A question or suggestion I received and the change I want to make.

Is my work easy to understand? What explanation, support or alternative would help my audience?

Depending on the format, explain images, provide text for audio or give an accessible explanation. Present information clearly. Mark anything that does not apply to your work as ‘Not applicable’.

Finish the work we agreed

What have I done? What example can I share of something I understood or created?

What helped me? What question or next step do I want to note?

After discussing the work with my teacher, note the tasks we reviewed and what is still to do or how we will continue.

Reviewing the agreed tasks completes the route. A route can be completed with the selected islands and pieces earned so far.

Our route and the piece collection

On the website: in early years and the first primary cycle, completing story comprehension automatically earns a piece. In other stages, learners also complete the true or false challenge. Teachers and learners review creative responses together.

In the ELPX: complete the passport by hand after reviewing the agreed tasks. Web progress, the ELPX passport and this sheet are kept separately.

The passport features seven modern pottery models and a shell for La Graciosa. The pintaderas are decorative and provide a historical reference. They are not assigned to individual islands.

The optional games ‘Island names’ and ‘The pieces and their islands’ help you practise. Play them to explore what you have learned, and discuss your work with your teacher. They do not award pieces or points or set your route.
