Activities across learning stages · web edition 4.9.4 · 1 November 2026. Choose curriculum links, tasks and support for your group. The stage settings give you a starting point. This guide describes the standalone website. The editable resource is available as version 4.8.9.

Choose how to experience the story

On each island, Audio and text lets you read the story and listen to Zeus Plasencia Carballo. You can turn the quiet background sounds on or off and adjust the reading settings. Video shows the illustrated story with its narration. English captions are available in the player controls and start off. The Download story (PDF) button remains visible in both views.

In Teacher → Activity guide you will find links to plan the activity and open the printable workbook. The option to save or restore progress opens Settings and progress. The website and the editable ELPX keep separate progress records.

Using MitoMapa in the classroom

Start with one island and a conversation. Each story works as a standalone activity. Start with any island and work with as many as you need. Choose according to the group’s interests, your time and the topic you are teaching.

Choose the length and agree a route

  • One session of about 45 minutes. Choose one island. Read or listen and choose a short task to share.
  • Short route in four sessions of about 50 minutes. Choose two islands, compare the stories, check a source and develop a creative response.
  • Optional collection in eight to ten sessions of 35–50 minutes. Choose a range of islands and workshops. Adjust the timing for tasks, breaks and support. Work with as many islands as suit the group.

Select islands in Teacher → Settings and progress and agree on the tasks to review. On each island, ‘Our work today’ explains the task and links to the Workshop and ‘Create and share’. You can complete a route through one or two islands even if you have pieces left to collect. All eight islands remain open. The suggested timings await a classroom trial.

Learning aims

Locate an island and its story, understand what happens to the characters and explain their decisions. With suitable support, learners can also distinguish fictional elements from information supported by sources and respond by speaking, writing, drawing or performing.

Prepare the group and workshop

Before you begin

  1. Choose the island. Read the story and review its themes and sources before introducing it to your group.
  2. Choose the learning stage and prepare for reading. On Home, select a stage to see suggested activities. In each Story, open ‘Reading settings’ to change or reset the text size, line spacing and text width. These settings apply across stages and are saved in this browser where local storage is available.
  3. Agree on a task and how to group learners. Read with the whole group, then continue in pairs, teams or individually. Partners can take turns using the website and explaining what they notice.
  4. Prepare the Workshop. In Settings and progress, enable the sections you will use. You can agree on an oral response, work on paper or another activity away from the website.

Example session

This example takes about 45 minutes. Adjust the timings, leave out an activity or spread the plan over two sessions.

A flexible session plan for one island
StageWhat to do with the group
Choose and locate · 5 minOpen Islands and choose Map or List. The list appears first on mobile. Both views offer all eight islands. Find the chosen island and ask learners what they know about it or what the question on its card makes them think of.
Story · 12 minRead or listen with the group. Pause to predict what might happen, explain unfamiliar words and discuss how the characters feel.
Challenges · 8 minAnswer questions and discuss clues in the text. If learners are unsure, go back to the story and ask them to explain which part helped them.
Workshop · 15 minChoose a vocabulary, research or creative task. Offer a starting point and give learners space to make their own choices.
Share · 5 minInvite learners to show their work, describe something that changed their mind or ask a question they still have. Agree on a next step.

Story → Challenges → Workshop provides a suggested sequence. You can revisit a section, pause for discussion or use only the parts you agreed on. The Workshop is open to everyone.

Support reading and creative work

Support the activity

  • Use the listening and reading controls. In Audio and text, you can adjust the reading settings and listen to Zeus Plasencia Carballo. Select Play to start the recording. You can pause, change the speed or turn off Quiet background sounds. The browser remembers your choice when it can save settings. Pupils can read or listen independently.
  • Allow time for comprehension. Read aloud, revisit passages and explain vocabulary. Let learners speak, point or draw before writing.
  • Ask pupils to explain their choices. In Challenges, open ‘Help to think and explain’ to find ‘Clue 1’, ‘Clue 2’ and ‘Back to the story passage’. Open ‘See explanation’ when helpful. Invite learners to explain why they chose one option and ruled out another, using words from the passage. They can speak or make notes in the workbook. Use their explanations to guide discussion. The questions do not record self-assessment or award pieces.
  • Compare the story with a source. The stories and illustrations are modern recreations. In Research, ‘How to check a source’ helps learners identify its author or institution, title and link or page. They can compare a claim and identify what the source cannot establish. Use three prompts aloud, on paper or in the optional field. ‘The story says… The source says… We are still unsure about…’ Checking the supporting questions and reviewing the comparison with a teacher are separate steps.
  • Help pupils create and review their work. ‘Create and share’ can start from one or two islands. It suggests an audience, gives an example with notes and offers questions for review. Provide a sentence starter, an outline or a scene learners can adapt. They can speak, write, draw or perform, share a draft in the agreed format and explain one change they made after reviewing it. The activity is available throughout the route. Learners share their work through the agreed channel.

The recordings preserve the original narration. Links to passages open the relevant text. Paragraph-by-paragraph playback would need separate alignment and human review.

Adapt the activity to each learning stage
  • Early years and first primary cycle: supported reading, a repeated refrain, a short discussion and a drawing or an oral retelling. Read the questions with learners who need support.
  • Second primary cycle: shared reading, vocabulary work and a brief explanation of the characters’ actions.
  • Third primary cycle: check a detail against a source and create a response that uses an example from the story to explain the choices made.
  • Secondary: compare interpretations and sources, explain causes and consequences and develop an extended response with the agreed length and support.

Use these suggestions as starting points. Choose tasks and support for your group. Learners can take the time they need, pause, try again and use support to demonstrate their learning.

Choose games to extend the activity or change the pace
  • Map, list and clues Use the Map/List switch to choose among the same eight islands. Enlarge the map while keeping its geography in view. The list shows each title and a question to get started. On mobile, the list appears first. Learners can use a clue, try again, skip to another question or open the story at any point.
  • Island names. match seven historical names and the ‘No documented name’ card to the map. Drag a card or select and place it using touch, a click or the keyboard. Compare the historical names with today’s names and discuss what the sources establish and what remains uncertain.
  • Puzzle and orientation in words rebuild the map in pairs or use ‘Locate the islands in words’, a text alternative to dragging. Show the solution or use the reference for support. The location clues are approximate and give no distances or navigation routes.
  • Put the story in order At the end of Story, open the optional activity to put four events in order and explain what happens before and after each event.
  • Compare two stories In Games, choose two islands and discuss one similarity and one difference. Learners can save a short reflection or talk through their comparison.

Use the names game as optional practice. The game has no grades, awards or route requirements. Its antique-style chart is a modern design based on the island geography. Before using it, check the sources and cautions for these names.

Save progress and understand how pieces are earned

Use saved progress to pick up where you left off

The browser remembers the work completed here. Save a copy to continue on another device. In Teacher → Settings and progress, choose the islands and tasks for your route. Discuss the evidence with learners and mark each island as reviewed. You can review both islands in a two-island route while the piece collection is still in progress. Review the evidence again if you change a task or a response.

The agreed route shows which islands learners have reviewed with their teacher. The collection of pieces appears separately. Discuss the conversation, research and creative work with learners. Completing the route records completion of the agreed work and gives no points or automatic grades.

The interactive Workbook remembers checked vocabulary, checked research questions, creative work marked as ready, drafts and qualitative review checklists. A saved draft or a response marked as ready still needs to be reviewed with a teacher. The source note is optional. Learners can discuss the comparison aloud or complete it away from the website. Save any progress you need and clear it before another learner or group uses the browser.

In early years and the first primary cycle, completing the comprehension task earns a piece. In other stages, learners complete the true or false challenge as well. Once they earn all eight pieces for the selected stage, a generic diploma opens automatically. It contains no learner names and can be printed or saved from the passport. The final challenge is optional. Learners can choose any island and take part in a session at any point.

Curriculum example for a short route

The GobCan guide gives an example for the third cycle of Spanish primary education. It starts with the sixth year and can be adapted for the fifth. It uses two islands in four sessions of about 50 minutes. For another group, select and check relevant curriculum competencies and assessment criteria before agreeing on tasks.

  1. Choose the first island, find it on the map and listen to its story. Discuss a decision one of the characters makes.
  2. Read the second story and compare the characters, conflicts or landscapes.
  3. Check one detail against a source and create a museum piece with a narrative, a fact supported by evidence and credits.
  4. Review and share the piece with the group. Propose one action to care for local heritage.

Record evidence, progress and a next step in the qualitative review. The review has no scores, percentages or grades. Use the games for practice and use learners’ work to inform your assessment. A classroom trial with learners is still pending.

On the website: match the pieces to their islands

In the optional activity Match the pieces to their islands , match the seven vessels and the shell for La Graciosa to the islands they represent in MitoMapa. Drag or select them using a click, touch, the keyboard or a drop-down list. The reference shows the matches without recording them as answers. Use clues and try again as needed. The game gives no scores or awards. It leaves the passport and route tasks unchanged. Answers reset when the page is reloaded.

The images are modern interpretations informed by sources. The shell is a contemporary symbol for La Graciosa. The game uses associations from MitoMapa. Consult the sources to understand what is documented about pottery on each island. The activity is available on the standalone website. The ELPX 4.8.9 does not include it.
